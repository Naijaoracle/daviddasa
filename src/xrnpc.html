Software engineering/XR healthcare simulation

XRNPC
Beyond scripted characters.

An event-driven intelligence system that allows virtual clinical colleagues to respond to what is happening within a healthcare simulation, rather than simply following a predefined script.

Designed to extend existing immersive simulation experiences through more context-sensitive character interaction.

01 / The challenge

Clinical encounters rarely follow a script.

In a clinical simulation, a learner may act earlier than expected, overlook an emerging problem, ask an unexpected question or change direction as new information becomes available. The other members of the simulated team need to make sense of those changes.

Traditionally scripted non-player characters can be effective in controlled scenarios, but their responses are often tied to anticipated triggers and fixed dialogue sequences. That can make it difficult to represent the fluid nature of teamwork in an unfolding clinical situation.

The central challenge is not to make a character say more. It is to make its response relevant to the moment, the patient and the rest of the team.

02 / The approach

Introducing XRNPC

XRNPC brings an event-driven approach to virtual character behaviour in XR healthcare simulation. It is designed to connect character interactions with events and conditions within the wider simulation, allowing responses to reflect the situation as it unfolds.

Rather than treating dialogue as a sequence detached from the environment, the system relates virtual colleagues' interactions to the learner's decisions, simulated patient changes and the activity of the clinical team. The aim is to support characters that participate more meaningfully in the scenario while remaining bounded by its clinical context and rules.

03 / What it enables

More responsive virtual clinical teams

The value of an event-driven character is experienced through its behaviour. Several characteristics matter especially in simulated team-based care.

01

Awareness of changing context

Character responses can take account of relevant developments in the scenario, rather than relying on a single predetermined conversational path.

02

Meaningful team interaction

Virtual colleagues can respond to requests, participate in simulated clinical activity and adapt their contributions as the situation changes.

03

Behaviour within boundaries

Flexible interaction remains situated within the constraints of the scenario, helping distinguish plausible character behaviour from unrestricted model output.

05 / Existing simulation environments

Extending what simulation environments can do

Immersive healthcare simulations already provide virtual spaces, clinical scenarios and opportunities for practice. XRNPC is designed to build on those experiences by adding more dynamic behaviour to the characters who inhabit them.

This approach places the emphasis on enriching interactions within an existing simulation experience rather than requiring a wholly new virtual environment.

The wider significance

Towards more adaptive healthcare simulation

Healthcare teamwork depends on more than the actions of any single participant. It involves communicating under uncertainty, noticing changes in a patient's condition, responding to requests and adapting to others. Virtual characters that can engage with this changing context offer a different way to design immersive simulation experiences.

XRNPC reflects a broader engineering question: how can intelligent character behaviour make a simulation more responsive without losing the structure and safeguards that clinical training requires? Its focus is on that intersection between adaptive interaction and the demands of a clinical scenario.
