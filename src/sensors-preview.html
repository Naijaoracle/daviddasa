The measurement pipeline

Digital sensing begins with a physical phenomenon, not with an algorithm. Each stage can introduce uncertainty and bias.

Cardiac electricity, blood volume, sound, motion or tissue structure.

Electrodes, photodiodes, microphones or imaging detectors.

03 / The supporting hardware

Where does the ESP32 fit?

The ESP32 is a microcontroller, not a physiological sensor. It connects to sensing hardware, reads digital or digitised signals, timestamps samples, and can perform basic processing or transmit data to another system.

For example, a MAX30102 can communicate with an ESP32 over I²C, while an AD8232's conditioned analogue output can be sampled by a suitable ADC. Neither arrangement turns the microcontroller itself into a sensor.

Sensor interfaceI²C, SPI or analogue inputs, depending on the hardware
Sampling and timingRates, buffers, timestamps and synchronisation
Edge processingFiltering, basic features and quality checks
Data transferSerial, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth where supported
04 / Interpretation

What a sensor cannot tell us

Measurement quality and clinical validity are separate questions. A clean waveform does not automatically imply an accurate diagnosis.

01 / SIGNAL ≠ STATE

The measurement is not the patient

ECG observes electrical activity; PPG observes optical changes; microphones observe sound. None independently captures the complete cardiovascular state.

02 / CONTEXT MATTERS

Performance can vary

Motion, placement, lighting, pigmentation, noise and measurement protocols can alter what reaches the algorithm and how reliably it can be interpreted.

03 / VALIDATION FIRST

An estimate needs evidence

Heart rate, blood pressure and other clinical outputs inferred from signals require suitable reference standards, uncertainty assessment and population-level validation.
